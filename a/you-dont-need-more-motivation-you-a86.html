Why you care about something and still don’t do it

By Nikesh Lagun

It’s not procrastination, it’s a failure to amplify the signal that starts motion.

Share

Post on X ↗LinkedIn ↗Copy link ↗

Hey friends,

Some days, you care deeply about something, maybe a project, a conversation you need to have, or a piece of work you’ve been meaning to start. You’re not resisting it. You’re not confused. It even matters to you.

And yet, nothing happens.

You sit with it. You feel it lingering. Still, no action comes.

That’s not laziness. It’s not classic procrastination either. What you’re bumping into is a structural issue: the system that turns caring into motion isn’t firing the way it needs to.

This is where CAP comes in. CAP stands for Cognitive Activation Potential. Think of it as your mind’s internal voltage amplifier. It’s not the spark itself that would be Primode, the ignition threshold, but CAP is the force that boosts the signal. It determines how much energy your system can actually deliver when you’re poised to act.

CAP takes whatever motivation or urgency is present and scales it. If Primode is the engine starter, CAP is the voltage that makes sure the ignition has enough force to actually start the engine. Without CAP, your intent may still be present, but it doesn’t move the system.

So what does this feel like?

You’re clear on the goal. You’re not avoiding it. You even want it. But your system is flat. There's no felt urgency. No internal lift. The task feels emotionally distant, and your body doesn’t follow through.

That’s a low-CAP state.

CAP isn’t the same as hype, and it doesn’t have to be dramatic. Even a small surge, one emotional click, or one moment of internal clarity can be enough to boost your system above the threshold. That’s the key: it doesn’t have to be high. It just has to be real.

And CAP doesn’t live in isolation. It’s part of a broader structure, what I call Lagunian Dynamics, a system that models how Drive arises (or fails to) based on internal alignment.

Here’s how it works:

  • Primode sets the threshold for ignition.

  • CAP determines how much emotional voltage is available to modulate that ignition.

  • Flexion shapes how well the task fits your current cognitive state.

  • Anchory tethers your attention.

  • Grain adds friction: resistance, hesitation, and emotional drag.

  • And Slip introduces unpredictability: system noise, mood shifts, random spikes, or dropouts.

In this structure, Drive isn’t about motivation alone. It’s an emergent output. And CAP plays a crucial role: it boosts the system’s ability to act if ignition is possible.

So if you’re in that state where you care but can’t move, here’s a better question than “Why am I procrastinating?”

Ask instead: Is CAP present?

Then try to engage it:

  • Reconnect emotionally: Why does this matter to you right now, not in theory, but personally?

  • Shrink the step: What’s the smallest motion you could make? Open the document. Say the first sentence aloud. Touch the edge of the task.

  • Feel it physically: Stand. Move. Let your body register your intent.

  • Anchor the action while that voltage is still live.

Don’t wait for the big surge. You’re not trying to get pumped; you’re trying to catch a live signal and let it carry you forward.

CAP doesn’t need to be massive.
It just needs to be enough.

Wishing you a lighter, easier week ahead,
