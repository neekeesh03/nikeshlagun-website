301 Million People Struggle with Anxiety: How to Feel in Control and Overcome Last-Minute Jitters

By Nikesh Lagun

Do you know?

Roughly 301 million people—an estimated 4% of the global population—have an anxiety disorder.

This is because of the work time frame, overtime, financial difficulties, etc. Having anxiety is such a burden; it not only affects our workflow but also affects our mental health.

Even though I had been prepared for weeks, I was still somewhat anxious.

My pulse was pounding so hard that I had trouble concentrating on the notes. I realized my hands were shaking when I realized the importance of the presentation. For a while, I even thought of faking illness and skipping it, but I realized I couldn’t do that either.

Therefore, I decided to take a short walk so that I could get some fresh air and get a break.

It was not fully eradicated, though; however, it was a relief to think that I had some measure of control over it.

At the time of presenting, I was still nervous, but it turned out much better than I expected it to be.

That was the time when I didn’t have any idea how to overcome anxiousness. I used to either take a walk or sing in my head to concentrate my mind. Later on, going to different books and articles gave me numerous ideas on ‘Last-minute jitters.’

If you ever feel your anxiety is hitting you harder, then use this technique called ‘Transferring Responsibilities’. This not only makes you feel better but also helps you to stay calm and focus on your target.

  1. Be comfortable—you can either sit or even lie—at least try to find a position where you can be relaxed and then close your eyes.

  2. Inhale for three or four deep breaths. For instance, when entering a room or when taking a deep breath, imagine that you are inhaling fresh air and that it is going all the way to the top of your head. Just as you exhale, that air rushes through, taking away all those things one has to worry and be concerned about.

  3. Select something to hold on to—perhaps God, luck, the universe, or whatever one would wish to. It may be worthwhile to look at it in terms of energy, as with other categories on the website.

  4. Now for a moment, picture yourself and all those things that are so sickeningly heavy—today’s meeting, for example, or that conversation you have to have—and deposit it all on the nicely arranged shoulders of this kindly disposed entity.

Imagine shedding out all your responsibilities! How light you are when a burden is no longer something that you have to endure. It is fully managed, and you barely have to do anything.

So what’s the idea?

That you should just chill and catch glimpses of good things now and then?

This trick might not work at first, but try it all the time when your breath rushes and your heart pounds harder.

You can also use another strategy—rewriting reality—to overcome the heaviness of your incomplete stories. The researchers claimed choosing to swap the belief was a far more effective solution than attempting to ignore it.

Say you’re heading for your class; someone just jostled you and passed without apology.

How would you feel? Your brain will replay that moment time and often during the class, too. Various thoughts of not being apologized will haunt you, and you will lose your concentration.

The only thing you can do to concentrate on your class will be to rewrite reality.

Move on to the next situation by assuming he was late for the class to even apologize, or the principal might call him. He might also beg pardon later. Choosing to rewrite perception will be the smartest thing to do.

It can help you get back to the recent moment and the right mental state to improve your performance.

Again, if you get lost in the past thoughts or your anxiety is at its peak, use these techniques to get relief to a certain level.

Distract your mind by doing something you love, whether it's painting, reading, or playing a game. Connect with a friend, cousin, or anyone who brings you joy. Their presence can help ground you at the moment.

Laughter is a great way to shift your mindset.

Enjoying a funny video can bring you back to the present and lighten your mood.

Make sure to utilize these techniques to enlighten your week.

Have a great week,
