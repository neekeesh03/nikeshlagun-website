How I saved myself from ruining my days

By Nikesh Lagun
In this article✨ My Top Pick for This Week💭 Quote of the Week

Share

Post on X ↗LinkedIn ↗Copy link ↗

Hey guys,

Trying hard to get productive, right?

Earlier, I discussed the experimental week, mentioned some of my habit formation, and application of the 1% rule in my habits. If you haven’t checked it out, try utilizing 6 minutes (experimental week).

I found myself being distracted and procrastinating this week. For the first 3 days, I was unavailable due to various reasons, which I guess is not important to mention.

Then, after Thursday evening I realized, I had not planned for my weekly Sunday newsletter. So, in haste, I am writing these things.

So basically, this week I will just mention Parkinson’s law, which I discovered recently.

Parkinson’s law states that, “Work expands to fill the time allocated for its completion.” To explain this statement, let me give you an example.

As you guys know, I am fond of researching so, let me say if my professor gives me a topic to research and allocates the deadlines of a month. Now, I will plan the month by reviewing papers, exploring the hypothesis, drafting the paper, and peer-reviewing the final draft. Every section will be of each week so that I can write a paper smoothly.

But, it won’t happen as I planned. Here, Parkinson’s law applies. I will find myself procrastinating and playing video games or watching movies for the first few weeks. I will start the paper the last week with my full focus without the distractions, only to finish it on time. Not only that, but I don’t know how the paper will, but I know that it wouldn’t be as the professor would expect.

The cheat code for Parkinson’s law is to give ourselves a shorter deadline. If I was given a month as the deadline, I need to give myself a shorter deadline, like 3 weeks. This means I will force myself to finish it before my actual deadline. Afterward, the remaining week can be rewarded for completion of the paper by playing games or watching movies.

Thomas Alva Edison, the great scientist, used this law to force himself to finish the project on time. He used to share his project ideas in the press before it was started. This forces him to finish his project on time to preserve his reputation.

You also find Elon Musk bluffing his ideas in front of the people before he starts to initiate them. He also uses Parkinson’s law by giving a specific deadline.

So, you can use various techniques to set a specific deadline. It might be writing down them in a notebook or using apps like Google Calendar, Todoist, Superlist, etc.

Have a great week!
