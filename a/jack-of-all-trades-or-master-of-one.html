Jack of All Trades or Master of One? A Must-Know Lesson Before You Graduate!

Hey folks,

First of all, my warm wishes for the festive month.

Let’s dive into today’s insights.

My brother used to say, “Don’t try to become a jack of all trades and master of none.”

During high school, I eagerly involved myself in every activity that piqued my interest.

Then, what were the perks? I have a portfolio of diverse activities—research, music, dance, digital writing, social service, and many more.

After graduating, I was like, “What will I do now?”

Am I truly being a jack of all trades but a master of none?

I have gained a wealth of experience. However, my CV cannot fit all of it onto a single page.

But where is my real talent—my true destiny?

I am neither an ace at research nor music. All I can do is flex on my resume.

I also hear a lot of this: You can be anything you want to become if you just work hard enough.

So what’s the purpose of being Jack?

Let’s sort it all.

If you go to the hospital for neurosurgery, you probably want an old doctor thinking of holding a handful of experiences. So what does that mean?

Aren’t you searching for a master?

But if you’re the CEO of a big company, you might want someone versatile—someone who can juggle a bunch of tasks without getting stuck on small things, like fixing cables.

Here you chose a jack.

So what does this mean?

Debating on Jack and Master can be cool, approaching their ideologies. But you need to remember that learning new skills doesn’t lead to downfall. It only enhances your creativity.

See Ali Abdaal; despite being a medical practitioner, he had dozens of skills, including business, public speaking, fast typing, graphic designing, copywriting, etc. Not only this, he published himself as a productivity author. He embodies both the jack of all trades and the master of many skills—doesn’t he? While he strives to master his craft from his perspective, we may see him as already having achieved mastery.

What I want to make clear is that being a jack or a master differs from perspective. Make sure to be a jack, but make others feel you're the master.

Remember, you have no limitations.

My suggestion is that no knowledge goes in vain only if you have felt the pain.

Keep going, keep learning, and keep trying.

Have a great week!
