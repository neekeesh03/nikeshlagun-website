Stop copying productivity routines that don’t match your brain

By Nikesh Lagun

Lagun’s Law shows you how to create a workflow that fits your natural drive patterns.

Hey friends,

You know what? Some people swear by 5 a.m. routines. Others thrive on long “deep work” blocks. You try what they recommend, and maybe it works for a few days, until it doesn’t.

Then you’re left wondering why their system runs smoothly while yours sputters.

The truth is simple: there is no universal routine.

Every brain operates differently because every cognitive system is built differently.

Lagun’s Law captures this with a clear message: your productivity pattern is structural, not moral. When another person’s routine fails for you, it doesn’t mean you’re undisciplined. It just means your system is configured differently.

Instead of copying habits that don’t match your wiring, the smarter move is to understand how your own Drive Engine works.

What Lagun’s Law Explains

These variables interact constantly. They decide if you can begin a task, stay focused, recover after distraction, or collapse under pressure. And because everyone’s configuration is different, your best workflow will never look exactly like someone else’s.

Why Borrowed Productivity Systems Don’t Fit

Most productivity advice is based on one person describing what works for their configuration. If they have low Primode and strong Anchory, they’ll swear that long, uninterrupted work blocks are the secret. If they have high CAP bursts, they’ll preach intensity over consistency.

But if your Primode, Grain, or Slip behaves differently, trying to force their method can backfire. You’re not fighting laziness, you’re fighting architecture.

Lagun’s Law suggests a better approach: observe your system instead of importing someone else’s.

Two Quick Experiments to Understand Your Drive Engine

These simple at-home tests reveal how your variables operate.

1. Five-Minute Ignition Probe

What it shows: Primode and CAP

How to do it:

  1. Choose a tiny task you’ve been avoiding.

  2. Set a five-minute timer.

  3. Try to start. Notice what happens.

Interpretation:

  • You start immediately → low Primode, strong CAP.

  • You hesitate but eventually begin → moderate Primode.

  • You stay stuck despite wanting to start → high Primode.

If starting is consistently the hardest part, the issue is ignition, not motivation.

2. Anchory Drift Test

What it shows: Anchory, Flexion, and Slip

How to do it:

  1. Choose a neutral task.

  2. Work on it for 20 minutes.

  3. Track how often your attention drifts and how hard it is to return.

Patterns:

  • Minimal drift → strong Anchory.

  • Drift with an easy return → moderate Anchory + good Flexion.

  • Drift plus emotional tension → high Grain.

  • Random, jumpy shifts → Slip-driven variability.

These patterns tell you how stable your focus is and what tends to break it.

Applying Lagun’s Law to Daily Life

Once you understand your system, you can design routines that match your architecture.

1. If You Struggle to Start (High Primode)

Lower the barrier instead of trying to push harder.

  • Use micro-start tasks (30 seconds only).

  • Create “open the file and stop” rituals.

  • Break the first step into the smallest possible action.

Ignition is the goal, not progress.

2. If Your Motivation Comes in Bursts (High CAP)

Use bursts strategically.

  • Attack important tasks during surges.

  • Work in short sprints instead of long blocks.

  • Capture quick wins to build momentum.

You’re built for intensity, not endurance.

3. If Tasks Don’t Feel Like They Fit (Low Flexion)

Adjust the task to your current state.

  • Break ideas into mechanical steps.

  • Switch environments to match the task.

  • Change modalities (talk, sketch, outline).

Fit matters more than force.

4. If You Drift Easily (Weak Anchory)

Don’t rely on long-focus routines.

  • Use 10–15 minute cycles.

  • Keep a simple “return point” note visible.

  • Work with gentle anchors like music loops or timers.

Stability comes from structure.

5. If Everything Feels Heavy (High Grain)

Reduce friction before tackling effort.

  • Clear one small latent task.

  • Lower emotional stakes.

  • Break tasks into friction-free micro steps.

Grain drops when pressure drops.

6. If Your Performance Swings (High Slip)

Plan for variability instead of fighting it.

  • Keep flexible daily pacing.

  • Match difficult tasks to stable days.

  • Avoid self-judgment on off days.

Slip isn’t a flaw. It’s a property of your system.

Track Yours. Don’t Copy Theirs.

Your Drive Engine has a pattern.

It’s not random, and it’s not broken. Lagun’s Law gives you a way to read that pattern and build routines that match your real wiring.

Once you stop forcing yourself into someone else’s architecture, you can finally build a workflow that feels natural, sustainable, and genuinely yours.

Wishing you a lighter, easier week ahead,

Words in plain English
