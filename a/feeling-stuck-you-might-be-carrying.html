Feeling stuck? You might be carrying too much grain.

By Nikesh Lagun

Discover the invisible resistance holding you back.

Share

Post on X ↗LinkedIn ↗Copy link ↗

Hey friends,

Ever felt like the simplest tasks are harder than they should be? Even when you’re motivated, things just feel heavy. You sit down to work, but your mind drifts, you get distracted, or you just can’t start. What you might not realize is that this feeling of heaviness is due to something called Grain. Let’s dive into what it is, why it happens, and how you can reduce it to make life feel easier.

Grain is friction, the invisible resistance in your mind that makes everything feel harder. It’s not laziness or lack of motivation. Grain is the mental clutter, stress, and emotional baggage that pile up over time. Think of it like trying to walk through thick mud. The more friction (or Grain) you have, the harder it is to take a step. Grain shows up when:

  • You’re stressed

  • You’re emotionally drained

  • You have too many things on your mind

  • Your tasks feel too big or unorganized

We all know that getting things done takes energy. But the more Grain you have, the more energy you have to use just to get started. Imagine it like this:

  • Low Grain: You spend 1 unit of energy to make 1 unit of progress.

  • High Grain: You spend 5 units of energy just to make 1 unit of progress.
    That’s why sometimes even simple tasks feel exhausting; you’re battling all this hidden resistance.

The good news is, you can lower your Grain and make tasks feel easier. Here’s how to do it:

  • Get Rid of Unfinished Business: Unresolved tasks are one of the biggest sources of Grain. These tasks stay in the background of your mind, draining your energy without you realizing it. Once a week, write down everything that’s open or unfinished. These are things you’ve been putting off, like emails, chores, or decisions. Getting them out of your head will clear some of the mental clutter.

  • Reset Your Mind: Your mind needs regular “reset” breaks. When you’re pushing through stress and fatigue, you’re not giving your brain the chance to cool down and recover, which leads to more Grain. Before starting a big task, take a 1-minute break. Close your eyes, breathe deeply, and check in with yourself. Ask, “How am I feeling?” Then, choose if you need to ramp up (if you’re too tired) or calm down (if you’re too stressed). This little reset can lower your Grain and make things feel more manageable.

  • Give Yourself Transitions Between Tasks: Jumping straight from one task to another can create mental friction, especially when you’re moving between very different types of work. This extra switch costs you mental energy. Between tasks, take 30 seconds to pause, stretch, or breathe. This short “transition time” helps lower Grain by giving your mind a chance to reset and refocus.

  • Anchor Your Focus: When Grain is high, it’s easy for your mind to wander. Anchory is a mental tool that helps you stay focused, like a tether holding your attention to the task at hand. Before starting any task, say to yourself, “What exactly am I doing in the next 10 minutes?” Write it down or say it aloud. Having a clear, specific focus will anchor your mind and prevent distractions from piling on more Grain.

  • Allow Some “Slips”; Don’t Aim for Perfection: Sometimes, your mind will slip or drift. That’s normal! Trying to force yourself to stay perfectly focused can actually make Grain worse. If you find yourself distracted, gently bring your focus back without judgment. It’s okay to have moments of distraction. The key is to not let it escalate into full mental overload.

  • Rest and Recharge: Most people think of rest as something you do only after you’ve worked hard. But rest is actually a key part of lowering Grain. Without it, your mind never fully recovers, and the resistance keeps building. Schedule regular breaks throughout the day. It doesn’t have to be long, even 10 minutes of doing nothing (or something relaxing) can help lower Grain and reset your mind for the next task.

Here’s a quick routine to reduce your Grain every day:

  • List three things causing friction (tasks, worries, or emotional stress).

  • Label each one as controllable or not. Handle the controllable, and let go of the rest for now.

  • Take a few deep breaths and visualize yourself lightening the mental load.

  • Start with a small task to build momentum and focus on one thing at a time.

It’s easy to feel stuck or lazy when tasks feel impossible. But more often than not, you’re not dealing with a lack of motivation; you’re dealing with too much Grain. By following these steps, you can lower the mental friction and make your tasks feel more manageable. It’s about tuning your mental engine, so you can get things done with less effort. The next time you feel like you can’t get started, don’t blame yourself. Check your Grain, reset, and get moving.

Wishing you a lighter, easier week ahead,

Nikesh Lagun
