3 Lessons I Learned About Slowing Down to Move Forward

By Nikesh Lagun

Stepping Back doesn’t mean Stepping Out.

In this article✨ My Top Pick for This WeekEssential Resources for Your Adventure 🔗

Share

Post on X ↗LinkedIn ↗Copy link ↗

Hey friends,

Let me ask you something: what image of progress do you have when you think about them? Is it simply clearing tasks off a to-do list? Are you hitting goals faster than in the past? Or something less tangible, like you feel in sync with your life?

This was something a friend of mine recently gave me a new perspective on. They had climbed the corporate ladder for years, and then they did something unusual: They took a slower, more leisurely one. Their reasoning? "Sometimes, I am tired of feeling like I’m just running in place." That hit me.

We confuse constant motion with progress of any sort.

Here’s the thing: We’ve all been taught that success is about outputting as much as you can. Work harder. Do more. Be better. And more and more people are coming back to themselves, realizing that what they’re doing is a pretty relentless pace, and there’s very little room in this life that you actually get to live. And the crazy part? And research supports them—strategic pauses and slower paces usually yield better results, sharper ideas, and longer-lasting motivation.

If you’re considering rethinking your pace, here are a few ideas to try:

  • Create space to reflect: Time to set aside to ask yourself if what you are doing is still in alignment with your long-term goals.

  • Embrace intentional pauses: Time off is NOT wasted; time off is an opportunity to come back out and have fresh eyes.

  • Prioritize quality over quantity: This means choosing tasks and commitments that have real value instead of simply giving you time to busy yourself.

Shifting gears isn’t easy. Hustle everywhere says slow down feels counterintuitive. The truth, however, is that stepping back doesn’t mean stepping out. It’s not about keeping busy. It’s about choosing a pace that fits with your goals and values.

So, here’s the question I’ve been asking myself lately: Where am I, and can I keep my pace? Is this a sprint to burnout? The point isn’t doing more; it’s about doing it better. What about you? Have you ever paused to think about how fast you were going? Let’s talk about it.

Have a great week!

Nikesh Lagun
