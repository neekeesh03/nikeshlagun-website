Tip 1 · 15 seconds

The hand check

15
Ready? It takes 15 seconds.You only need your hand.

How did it feel?

A fun self-check from my research. Not a medical test.

Why it works

The task is odd on purpose. Your mind has no habit for it, so you see how it really behaves. Where it feels smooth, blocked or wobbly tells you something about your focus today.

  • Smooth: your mind follows your intentions
  • Blocked: a lot of inner friction right now
  • Mind jumped: your focus slipped mid-thought
  • Could not choose: your mind is wobbling
  • Relief when you moved: lots of push built up

Tip 5 · 12 seconds

The eye check

See how easily your focus locks back on after a small distraction.

12
Ready? It takes 12 seconds.Keep your head still. Only move your eyes.

How did it feel?

A fun self-check from my research. Not a medical test.

What you do

  • Look at the dot straight ahead (2 seconds)
  • Shift your eyes slightly right (2 seconds)
  • Shift slightly left (2 seconds)
  • Come back to the middle and hold (4 seconds)
  • Notice what stayed steady, drifted or felt tense (2 seconds)

Small left and right shifts gently disturb your focus. How quickly you settle shows how well your attention can lock back on.

Small habits that help

Tip 2 · Focus

Stretchy work blocks (20-2-20)

20 minutes of focused work.

2 minutes to change something: stand up, switch spots or switch tools.

20 more minutes of work.

In my research notes, adding a small change like this lifted sustained effort by over 25%. It balances focus with flexibility.

Tip 3 · Clear your head

Write down every unfinished task

Label each one:

Active: I'll do it today.

Later: I'll do it, with a date.

Let go: I'm not doing this.

This clears mental clutter and frees energy that was stuck worrying in the background.

Tip 4 · Start

The 5-minute start test

1. Pick a tiny task you've been avoiding.

2. Start a 5-minute timer.

3. Try to start, and notice what happens.

If starting is always the hardest part, the problem is getting going, not motivation. Make the task smaller.
