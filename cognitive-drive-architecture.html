Introduction

Cognitive Drive Architecture (CDA) is a proposed structural field within cognitive psychology that models Drive, the system's capacity to initiate, sustain, and regulate cognitive effort, as an emergent property of real-time system configuration, rather than a fixed motivational state or behavioral trait.

Why does effort sometimes fail to begin or collapse mid-task even when intention, knowledge, and motivation are in place?

It formalizes this question structurally, defining the internal conditions under which Drive is mechanically possible, and conversely, when effort failure must occur, regardless of desire or capacity.

Core Concept

Existing models in motivation, executive function, and attention describe correlates of effort, but not its structural architecture.

CDA asserts that Drive is not reactive, nor emergent from abstract states.

It is structural:

  • Generated by the alignment of six internal system variables
  • Governed across three operational domains
  • Predictive of specific failure patterns and performance variability

The Architecture of Drive

At the heart of CDA is Lagunian Dynamics, a first-principles theory of Drive formalized through Lagun's Law of Primode and Flexion Dynamics.

Domains of Effort:

Ignition

Governs initiation of action (Primode, CAP).

Tension

Governs sustained engagement and attentional stability (Flexion, Anchory, Grain).

Flux

Governs intra-individual variability over time (Slip).

Failure Patterns (Configuration States)

Low Primode

Start Failure

No ignition despite intention.

High CAP + inactive Primode

Motivated Stall

Volitional tension without action.

Low Flexion + High Grain

Load Collapse

Early burnout or dropout.

Low Anchory + High Grain

Anchor Fatigue

Attentional drift mid-task.

High Anchory + Low Flexion

False Focus

Persistent but non-productive focus.

High Slip

Volatility

Inconsistent performance across time.

High CAP + Primode = 0

Inverted Ignition

Paralysis under pressure.

Toward a Structural Science of Effort

CDA's central claim is that effort is not a trait.

It is a system output, mathematically legible and structurally testable.

As a field, CDA seeks to advance:

  • Precision modeling of effort variability
  • Cross-domain understanding of performance failure
  • Practical tools for optimizing Drive across contexts

This is a first-principles architecture, not a motivational toolkit. It models what must align internally for effort to begin, persist, or fail.
